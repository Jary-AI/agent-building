# Building a Useful SDR Agent

**Audience:** Jary Portal Academy
**Visibility:** Public learning asset
**Version:** 2026-10-07

## What this agent is for

An SDR agent should help a team decide who to speak with, why the conversation matters now, what useful value to offer and what should happen next.

It should prepare good decisions rather than produce more activity. A send, view or signup is only an intermediate signal. The meaningful result is a person taking a useful next step: bringing a real workflow, accepting a scoped discussion, asking for evidence, booking the right conversation or clearly choosing not to continue.

## The operating loop

```text
current signal
  -> choose one campaign
  -> understand the person and situation
  -> offer one useful next step
  -> prepare the message or asset
  -> human review
  -> small live test
  -> read the result
  -> improve the next decision
```

The loop is deliberately small. It lets a team learn which problem, audience and offer deserve more attention before adding volume.

## Two ways work enters

### When someone has already shown intent

They may subscribe, request an asset, book a session, reply to a message, join a programme or ask for help.

The agent identifies the request, gives a useful response from approved information and routes the person when judgement is needed. The person should receive value even when there is no immediate sales opportunity.

### When the team starts the campaign

The team may have a market signal, a new programme, a partner goal or a specific account hypothesis.

The agent first defines the audience, operating problem, reason to act now, useful offer and evidence. It then prepares the campaign for human review. Proactive work earns its right to arrive by being relevant and useful.

## The campaign contract

Before a campaign begins, answer ten questions:

1. What result are we trying to create?
2. Who is the audience and which roles are involved?
3. What situation or trigger makes the topic relevant?
4. What problem does the audience already recognise?
5. What is the smallest useful offer?
6. What evidence supports the message?
7. Which channel is best for this step?
8. What should the person do next?
9. Who owns the response and what is the stop condition?
10. How will we know whether to continue, change or stop?

If these answers are unclear, the campaign is still a hypothesis.

## How the agent thinks

The agent combines several useful disciplines:

- **Account-based thinking:** understand the organisation and buying group before choosing a contact.
- **Insight-led messaging:** teach one relevant operational insight, tailor it to the role and propose a practical next step.
- **Structured discovery:** understand the current situation, pain, impact, critical event and decision path.
- **Stage-appropriate qualification:** inspect deeper commercial details only after a real opportunity exists.
- **Readiness gates:** check the market, problem, offer, sales motion, enablement and control before a programme is published.

These methods are tools for better decisions. They are not scripts to recite.

## The evidence card

For every important account or signal, create a short evidence card:

```text
Who is involved?
What is happening now?
Where is the work or decision breaking down?
What is the consequence?
Why might it matter now?
Who decides what happens next?
What evidence supports this view?
What remains unknown?
What useful next step can we offer?
```

The unknowns matter as much as the knowns. They prevent confident language from hiding weak research.

## Why human judgement remains part of the design

The agent can research, compare, draft, check links, prepare questions and recommend a next action. A person reviews the exact audience, claims, message, timing and commitment immediately before an external action.

This protects the relationship and keeps responsibility clear. It also improves the agent: the human correction becomes evidence about what good judgement looks like, rather than an invisible edit lost in a conversation.

## What to measure

Measure the path from attention to useful progress:

```text
relevant reach
  -> useful engagement
  -> content or programme action
  -> human conversation
  -> qualified problem
  -> accepted scope or evidence step
  -> delivered value and repeatability
```

Also record negative signals: confusion, irrelevant messages, opt-outs, unsupported claims, excessive support effort and opportunities that should have stopped earlier.

## A practical example

A partner may be interested in AI but that is too broad to act on. A useful agent turns the interest into a bounded exercise:

> Bring one recurring customer workflow. We will map the current systems, the human decision, the smallest useful product route, the delivery responsibilities and the evidence needed for a first customer conversation.

The result is not a generic demo. It is a clearer problem, an accountable owner, a next decision and a way to learn whether the route can be repeated.

## How the agent improves

After each campaign, record:

- what we expected;
- what we did;
- what happened;
- what evidence supports the result;
- what changed in our understanding;
- what we will test next.

A tactic becomes reusable only after it is supported by explicit feedback, repeated evidence or a controlled comparison. A single successful message is a lead for investigation, not a new universal rule.

The strongest SDR agent is therefore not the one that sends the most messages. It is the one that helps the team make better choices, preserves trust and steadily turns real conversations into repeatable learning.
