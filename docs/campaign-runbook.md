# Campaign Runbook

This is the reproducible sequence for one SDR campaign. A campaign ends with a decision about the next test; it does not end when content is written.

## Step 0: Define the success evidence

Write one sentence:

> We will know this campaign is useful when **[named audience]** takes **[meaningful next action]**, producing **[business evidence]** by **[date]**.

Examples:

- A channel partner brings one customer workflow to a Partner Fit Clinic and accepts a dated discovery next step.
- An investor requests a specific evidence review or introduces a relevant operator.
- A Portal subscriber returns to an asset and books a route conversation.

Do not use “more awareness” or “more leads” as the only success statement.

## Step 1: Read the live system

Read in this order:

1. Jary Portal: programs, pipeline, assets, subscriptions, bookings, updates and current account activity.
2. CRM: read-only reconciliation of owner, stage, history and next step.
3. Product specifications and release evidence.
4. Branding and marketing sources.
5. Previous campaign and learning records.

Record date, source, scope and missing access. A public page, local branch, demo, draft or old deck cannot confirm a live capability by itself.

## Step 2: Choose one program

Select an existing Portal GTM Program when one already has a credible audience, offer and readiness state. Inspect its six gates and current enrolment before proposing a new program.

Selection score:

| Factor | Question |
|---|---|
| Result path | Can the program produce a named next decision? |
| Readiness | Are Market, Problem, Offer, Sales motion, Enablement and Control complete? |
| Capacity | Can Jary and the partner actually support the promised next step? |
| Evidence | Can the agent show approved proof without overclaiming? |
| Learning value | Will the test clarify a strategic uncertainty? |

## Step 3: Build the evidence card

```text
Account:
Persona / buying role:
Relationship and Portal state:
Trigger:
Situation:
Pain:
Impact:
Critical event:
Decision path:
Evidence:
Unknowns:
Exclusions / suppression:
Proposed offer:
Owner:
Next action and date:
Confidence:
```

Unknowns are part of the output. They tell the human what the next conversation must discover.

## Step 4: Choose the smallest offer

Select one of these before selecting channels:

- useful Portal asset;
- Partner Fit Clinic;
- customer workflow review;
- investor evidence brief;
- readiness review;
- scoped workshop;
- approved nurture item;
- meeting with a named decision purpose.

The offer must create value even if no sale follows. That is how the system earns trust and produces better evidence.

## Step 5: Design the channel journey

Give each channel one job:

| Channel | Job |
|---|---|
| Portal | Owned destination, knowledge, program entry, subscription, booking and opportunity state |
| Gmail | Relationship-specific, human-approved conversation |
| Brevo | Consent-based nurture and news |
| Metricool | Public proof, social awareness and measurement |
| Calendar | Convert an accepted purpose into a meeting option |
| HubSpot | Downstream reconciliation after human verification |
| GitHub / Wiki | Source, record and learn; never a prospecting channel |

No campaign should use every channel by default. Choose the minimum path that can test the hypothesis.

## Step 6: Draft the campaign packet

Create:

1. campaign contract;
2. audience and exclusion list;
3. evidence cards;
4. Portal destination and UTM/tracking plan;
5. channel-specific copy/assets;
6. reply and routing plan;
7. KPI and readback plan;
8. approval packet.

Channel adaptation changes the form, not the underlying claim. Email can ask a direct question; social can teach the pattern; Portal can provide the evidence and next action.

## Step 7: Run preflight QA

Check:

- audience, consent and suppression;
- source/status of every product or customer claim;
- language and meaning parity;
- links, tracking and destination state;
- mobile/readability and visual hierarchy;
- reply owner and escalation route;
- capacity and meeting availability;
- opt-out and unsubscribe path;
- Portal permissions and asset visibility;
- draft/publish status in the actual tool.

## Step 8: Apply the human gate

Show the exact recipients, exact content, destination, timing, claims and KPI. The approver can approve, edit, reject or park. Record the decision and reason.

## Step 9: Run the smallest live test

Use one cohort, one offer, one primary channel or tightly coordinated minimum set and a defined time window. Do not broaden because a post was published or a message was sent.

## Step 10: Read downstream results

Read the Portal and channel sources after the defined window. Look for:

- meaningful content action;
- reply quality;
- booking with a real purpose;
- named workflow or customer;
- accepted scope;
- evidence request;
- unsubscribe, objection or stop signal;
- founder/support effort required.

## Step 11: Decide and learn

Choose **continue**, **change**, **nurture** or **stop**. Record result, confidence, limitation and next date. Promote the lesson only when the learning criteria are met in the QA and learning loop.
