# Methodology and Reasoning

The agent uses several established methods for different decisions. The methods are complementary; none of them is a complete SDR operating system.

## Method map

| Decision | Method | What it contributes | When to use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Which accounts deserve attention? | ABM / Account-Based Everything | Account selection, buying-group mapping and coordinated touchpoints | Named partner, customer and investor motions |
| What should the message teach? | Challenger | A relevant operating insight that changes how the buyer sees a problem | Proactive outreach and thought leadership |
| What must be diagnosed? | SPICED | Situation, Pain, Impact, Critical Event and Decision | Discovery and qualification |
| How complex is the opportunity? | MEDDPICC | Metrics, economic buyer, decision criteria/process, paper process, pain, champion and competition | Only after human acceptance and when deal complexity justifies it |
| Is a Portal program ready? | GTM readiness gates | Market, Problem, Offer, Sales motion, Enablement and Control | Before a program can publish or open enrolment |
| Is a use case worth pursuing? | Jobs-to-be-Done lens | The job, progress sought, constraint and desired outcome | Customer workflow and product-route selection |

## 1. ABM: select the account before the contact

ABM is used to prevent random prospecting. The agent first identifies the account thesis, then the buying group and then the contact route.

The research sequence is:

1. Confirm the account exists in an approved segment.
2. Identify the likely operating situation and business consequence.
3. Map the roles involved: economic owner, operational owner, technical gatekeeper, user and possible champion.
4. Check existing Jary relationship, Portal status, CRM status, suppression and conflict.
5. State the trigger and what evidence is still missing.
6. Select the smallest account-specific offer.

Why it works: the account context supplies coherence across channels. A contact is not treated as an isolated email address.

## 2. Challenger: teach, tailor, take control

The agent uses Challenger as message structure, not as an aggressive script.

- **Teach:** name one overlooked operational consequence or useful pattern.
- **Tailor:** connect it to the role, installed base or current initiative using evidence.
- **Take control:** ask for a practical next decision with a clear scope and no false urgency.

Example pattern:

```text
observed operating tension
  -> consequence the role already cares about
  -> bounded route to inspect it
  -> one decision or meeting
```

Why it works: the recipient receives a useful point of view, not a product catalogue. It also limits the claim surface.

## 3. SPICED: diagnose before proposing

Use the five elements as questions and evidence fields:

- **Situation:** What happens today? Which systems, people and objects are involved?
- **Pain:** Where does work break, wait, repeat or lose ownership?
- **Impact:** What does the break cost in time, risk, revenue, customer promise or management attention?
- **Critical event:** Why does it matter now? What date, incident, renewal, launch or policy creates urgency?
- **Decision:** Who accepts the route, what evidence is required and what is the next decision date?

Why it works: it keeps discovery attached to a real operation. It avoids jumping from vague interest to a solution pitch.

## 4. MEDDPICC: use only when earned

MEDDPICC is a late-stage inspection tool. It is useful when an opportunity has multiple stakeholders, material spend, procurement or competitive risk. It is premature for an unqualified Portal visit or first reply.

The agent should expose missing fields rather than score a deal with invented certainty. A gap is a next question, not a reason to pretend the opportunity is mature.

## 5. Portal GTM readiness gates

The Portal program builder uses six gates:

| Gate | Required answer |
|---|---|
| Market | Who needs this, who decides and what signals indicate fit? |
| Problem | What pain exists in revenue, risk, time or operating terms? |
| Offer | What is provided, for whom, under what constraints and with what outcome? |
| Sales motion | What is the opener, discovery path, demo, objection route and next step? |
| Enablement | Which pitch asset, playbook and named owner support execution? |
| Control | Who owns the program, what are the dates, boundaries, measures and legal/brand controls? |

Why it works: the gates force campaign readiness to include delivery and control. Copy cannot hide a missing offer, owner or proof path.

## 6. Combining the methods

The sequence is:

```text
ABM selects the account
  -> Challenger frames the message
  -> SPICED structures the conversation
  -> MEDDPICC inspects accepted complexity
  -> GTM gates control the campaign
  -> outcome evidence decides what to repeat
```

The agent does not mechanically run every framework on every target. It selects the lightest method that resolves the current decision and records why a heavier method was deferred.

## 7. Reasoning discipline

For every claim, write one of four labels:

- **Observed:** directly read from a current source.
- **Reported:** supplied by a person or approved record.
- **Inferred:** a reasoned interpretation with the source and uncertainty shown.
- **Proposed:** a hypothesis or plan awaiting validation.

This simple taxonomy is one of the main reasons the agent can work safely in a changing company. It makes uncertainty visible instead of smoothing it into confident copy.
